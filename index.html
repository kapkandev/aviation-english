TRANSCRIPT EN

Before take-off

Before every flight, the pilots and cabin crew complete a series of safety checks. The flight crew review the weather, calculate the amount of fuel required, and inspect the aircraft systems. After boarding, cabin crew make sure all passengers are seated and their seat belts are fastened. When air traffic control gives clearance, the aircraft taxis to the runway. The engines increase their power, the aircraft accelerates, and the nose lifts into the sky. A safe flight begins with careful preparation and clear communication.

03 / BUILD YOUR WORD BANK

Vocabulary

Eight essential words to help you describe the aircraft, the crew, and the journey.

01

Aircraft

A vehicle designed for air travel, such as an aeroplane or helicopter.

“The aircraft is ready for boarding.”
02

Runway

A long, level surface where aircraft take off and land.

“Flight 204 is approaching the runway.”
03

Cabin crew

Staff who look after passengers and safety inside an aircraft.

“The cabin crew welcomed everyone on board.”
04

Altitude

The height of an aircraft above sea level or the ground.

“We are flying at an altitude of 10,000 metres.”
05

Take-off

The moment when an aircraft leaves the ground and begins to fly.

“Please fasten your seat belt for take-off.”
06

Air traffic control

A service that manages aircraft movement in the air and on the ground.

“The pilot contacted air traffic control.”
07

Fuselage

The main body of an aircraft, where the crew and passengers sit.

“The wings are attached to the fuselage.”
08

Turbulence

Unsteady air movement that can make an aircraft move suddenly.

“The captain announced a short period of turbulence.”
